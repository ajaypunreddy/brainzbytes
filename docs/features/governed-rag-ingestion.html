Non-negotiable outcomes

Freshness and governance are runtime invariants, not background aspirations.

1Sources remain in storage owned by a user, group, or organization.
2Access is delegated by reference with the least privilege needed for an approved purpose.
3Events reduce lag, while periodic reconciliation guarantees eventual detection of missed change.
4Changed, deleted, revoked, quarantined, or legal-hold sources are immediately ineligible.
5New index generations remain dark until every security, quality, lineage, and compatibility gate passes.
6Publication is an atomic logical-alias switch, never an in-place mutation of the active index.
7Active work pins exact source, policy, service, contract, parser, chunker, model, and index versions.
8Old and new service versions overlap so upgrades and approved downgrades do not interrupt work.
Distributed-systems reality: absolute zero propagation delay is impossible. The enforceable guarantee is that once ContextWeaver knows a revision is stale or access has changed, that projection cannot be retrieved.

Source Catalog + Freshness Ledger

Revision equality is necessary. Authorization is still decisive.

Queryable-state invariant
state == current
AND sourceRevision == indexedRevision
AND current ACL and purpose decision permits access
AND generation, schema, and model versions are allowed
AND no revocation, deletion, quarantine,
    or legal-hold tombstone applies
CurrentThe indexed revision exactly matches the observed source and all current policy checks permit retrieval.
StaleA newer source or ACL revision is known. Exact-freshness retrieval blocks until a validated generation is published.
DeniedRevocation, deletion, quarantine, legal hold, classification, residency, retention, or purpose rules make the source ineligible.
Events optimize detection latency. Reconciliation supplies correctness by comparing stable IDs, revisions, hashes, ACL snapshots, classification, retention, and deletion state.

Twelve gates stand between a source reference and a queryable generation.

IdentityVerify connector identity, source authority, tenant, principal, and approved purpose.
Resource limitsConstrain file type, size, parser, archive depth, decompression, and execution budgets.
MalwareScan content and containers before any parser or transformation can consume them.
Classification + DLPDetect handling constraints and assign the most restrictive applicable classification.
Regulated dataDetect secrets, PII, PHI, PCI, export controls, and other governed categories.
Prompt injectionFind invisible instructions, hidden text, coercive content, and tool-manipulation attempts.
Trust + poisoningEvaluate anomalies, duplicates, source reputation, provenance, and poisoning indicators.
Rights + consentValidate licensing, copyright, consent, retention, and allowed AI use.
Immutable commitCommit normalized artifacts and hashes without turning projections into a source of truth.
Versioned transformPin parser, extractor, chunker, tokenizer, embedding, and index-schema versions.
Publication proofValidate counts, hashes, ACL filters, lineage, quality, poisoning, and reader compatibility.
Publish or quarantineAtomically promote the complete generation or isolate it with an auditable reason.
Inheritance rule: vectors, lexical indexes, entities, summaries, caches, memory references, and ContextPacks inherit the classification and lifecycle of their source.
Dimensional precedence

There is no universal organization > group > user hierarchy.

Governance first determines what is eligible. Application ranking operates only inside that authorized set.

DimensionResolution rule
AuthorizationExplicit deny and missing purpose authorization win before any similarity search.
Mandatory policyApplicable regulatory and organization obligations cannot be removed by group, workflow, service, or user configuration.
Content authorityThe designated owner or system of record wins for the facts it governs.
PersonalizationUser preferences may override defaults only when no legal, security, or organizational obligation conflicts.
ClassificationThe most restrictive applicable handling and disclosure requirement wins.
RetentionLegal hold wins; otherwise the approved governing retention policy applies.
ResidencyThe most restrictive applicable location rule controls storage and processing.
Workflow policyA workflow can narrow authority and context, but it cannot expand them.
RankingTask relevance and preference weights affect only already-authorized candidates.
Effective Retrieval Plan

Every query becomes an immutable, auditable retrieval contract.

Identity: tenant, principal, roles, groups, and delegated authority
Purpose: service, workflow, prompt intent, and allowed use
Authority: mandatory sources, systems of record, and conflict rules
Eligibility: ACL, freshness, classification, retention, residency, and consent

Seamless upgrades and safe downgrades

Aliases move. Active work does not.

New work resolves stable aliases to promoted immutable versions. Existing workflows and ingestion jobs finish on their pinned version graph.

1. ExpandRegister candidate contracts and apply backward-compatible, expand-only schema changes.
2. OverlapStart new replicas while old replicas remain ready and continue pinned work.
3. ProveRun synthetic, shadow, mixed-version, canary, rollback-readability, and policy tests.
4. PromoteAtomically move service and index aliases for newly compiled work.
5. DrainRetire old versions only after active runs and queues reach zero and the rollback window closes.

Legal holdPreservation never implies AI retrieval permission or broader disclosure.

Human stewardshipOwnership, dispute handling, correction, approval, and accountable exception paths.
Version driftDetect model, parser, tokenizer, chunker, embedding, and index-schema drift.
Disposable projectionsIndexes are rebuilt from authoritative sources and lineage rather than treated as irreplaceable truth.
